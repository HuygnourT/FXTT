Research process

Ten stages, in order. A broker does not receive a score until stage eight is complete for at least one country.

01

Broker selection

We prioritise brokers by reader demand in each market, not by commercial relationship.

Research queue

02

Legal entity identification

From the client agreement and the sign-up flow, we find which company actually onboards residents of each country.

Entity map

03

Regulatory verification

We look the entity up on the regulator’s own register and match name, licence number and permissions.

Licence record

04

Account opening

A researcher opens a live account as a resident, completing the same verification as a normal client.

KYC record

05

Trading condition analysis

We measure spreads, commissions, swaps and execution across at least five sessions.

Cost log

06

Deposit testing

At least three deposits through the methods locals actually use, each timed from payment to credit.

Deposit records

07

Withdrawal testing

At least three withdrawals, timed from request to funds received, with fees recorded.

Withdrawal records

08

Country-specific verification

We confirm that what we tested applies to the country we publish for: entity, leverage, payments and language.

Country report

09

Editorial review

A second editor checks every claim against its evidence before scores are calculated.

Review sign-off

10

Publication

The review, score and redacted evidence go live together, with the test dates shown.

Published review

Rating framework

Six categories, each scored 0 to 5 from evidence, then weighted. The overall score is shown for one country at a time.

Score = sum of (category score x weight). A broker with incomplete tests shows “Pending” instead of a partial score.

Regulatory research

We trace six links from the brand you see to the rules that protect you. If one link cannot be verified, the review says so.

1. Broker brand

The marketing name

We list every website and app the brand operates in the region.

2. Legal entity

The company that holds your account

Taken from the client agreement shown to a resident during sign-up.

3. Regulator

Who supervises that entity

Confirmed on the regulator’s own website, never from the broker’s claims.

4. Licence

The register entry

Licence number, status and date matched against the entity name.

5. Permission

What the licence allows

Some licences do not cover retail CFDs or clients abroad. We check scope.

6. Jurisdiction

The law that applies

Where disputes are heard and which compensation scheme, if any, applies.

Testing methodology

A researcher based in the country uses a real account and real money. Every step is timestamped.

Account opening

Sign-up as a resident, noting which entity and terms are shown.

Records: screens, agreement version

KYC

Documents requested, rejections, and time to approval.

Records: timestamps, request log

Deposits

Three or more deposits through local and international methods.

Records: amount, fee, time to credit

Withdrawals

Three or more withdrawals back to the original method.

Records: request, approval, receipt

Trading platforms

Desktop and mobile, order placement and stability during news.

Records: platform notes, fills

Customer support

Scripted questions in the local language by chat and email.

Records: transcripts, response times

Local payment methods

QR payments, local bank rails and e-wallets that residents use.

Records: method availability by entity

Country verification

Brokers often run several legal entities. The one you are assigned depends on your country of residence, and it decides your leverage, your protections and your payment options.

That is why every review on this site asks for your country first, and why the same brand can have different test results in different markets.

Evidence standards

What counts as evidence

  • Screenshots of payment confirmations and platform balances
  • Transaction records from the bank or payment provider
  • Timestamps for every step, recorded in local time
  • Register lookups saved on the date of verification

What we always mask

  • Account numbers and trading account IDs
  • Names of account holders and bank staff
  • Bank details and card numbers
  • Identity documents and transaction identifiers

Editorial independence

No paid rankings. Brokers cannot pay for a review, a higher score or a position in any list or comparison table.

How we make money. We may earn a commission when a reader opens an account through some links. Those links are labelled wherever they appear.

Separation. The commercial team cannot see or edit scores before publication, and researchers are not told which brokers we have agreements with.

Our commitment

Commercial relationships do not determine ratings or research conclusions.

If a broker we have an agreement with performs poorly in testing, the review says so.

Update process

Broker conditions change. Every review shows its last reviewed date and a dated log of changes.
